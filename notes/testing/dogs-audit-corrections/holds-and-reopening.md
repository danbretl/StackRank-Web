# Editorial holds and reopening

The combined catalog keeps every stable ID, source-ID mapping, stored ranking/list entry and backup record. Editorial suppression is reversible and separate from prose/artwork approval. An evidence hold says that the current selectable card is not adequately supported; it does not say the animal, cross or historical name is fictitious. Lack of kennel-club recognition is not a reason to hold a responsibly evidenced domestic breed, variety, regional/historical type or cross.

The rebuilt candidate contains **806 public identities and 433 hidden identities**: 902 complete portrait/profile pairs, of which 96 are editorially suppressed, plus 337 incomplete entries. Cohort N contributes 60 public pairs and 40 held pairs. All 251 editorial holds remain reversible; 155 already-incomplete identities gain an explicit hold without changing present public visibility. The earlier 799-public candidate was provisional. Seven feasible cross rewrites were recovered before release; the final count is an outcome, not a quota.

## Reasons and exact reopening tests

| Category | What the decision means | Evidence and changes required before reopening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Redundant source identity | Two selectable cards currently denote the same bounded population; historical names/spellings alone do not make a second dog type. Examples include Japanese Akitainu versus the already Japanese-scoped Akita, and Miniature German Spitz versus the reviewed Klein. This is a selection decision, not an identity merge. | Establish a genuinely distinct population, historical scope, registry variety or selected strain for this exact source node and explain how it differs from the retained card. Provide source-specific naming evidence and a useful independently reviewed profile/portrait. If it remains the same population, retain its stable hidden ID and lookup/history; do not reopen a duplicate merely with different prose or silently remap saved entries. |
| Broad umbrella or unspecified function | Hound, Retriever, Setter, Sheepdog, Spaniel, Terrier, Water Dog and Water Spaniel encompass materially different breeds or jobs. Accurate group history alone does not make one representative portrait an exact dog type. | Demonstrate a bounded, useful domestic type or regional working family with a coherent scope distinct from existing member cards, and label the breadth explicitly. Sources must justify the representative morphology and profile without implying a uniform recognized breed or personality. Do not narrow an unqualified source ID to one arbitrarily chosen member. The four retained regional working families show that broad scope is not automatically disqualifying. |
| Administrative or unbounded category | Data-entry placeholders and an unrestricted Mixed Breed category do not specify one population. They remain valid ontology/history records. | Supply exact source evidence that this ID denotes a bounded population rather than an administrative catch-all, plus a useful profile and representative portrait. Parentage, history or morphology may support that account; a generic “all dogs vary” paragraph cannot resolve an unbounded identity. Otherwise retain the category outside new selection. |
| Wild species or unbounded wild/domestic cross | Wild-species entries are outside the domestic Dogs catalog. Coydog and the broad Wolfdog category also lack a bounded domestic type; one sanctuary animal cannot define their morphology or character. This does not reject evidenced domestic breeds with historical wolf ancestry. | For a source-scope error, establish that the exact ID actually denotes a bounded domestic population and correct the interpretation without repurposing a saved identity. Otherwise a product scope change would require fresh authority, not just better prose. Any future supported scope still needs exact population evidence and a new profile/portrait review; no single-animal rescue or sanctuary account can establish cross-wide temperament. |
| Unresolved exact population or conflicting name | A name may denote different regional populations, a disputed registry scope, a syndrome/colour rather than a population, or different parentages. Examples include Labrador Husky/Huskador, Landseer, Maltese Terrier, Leopard Cur and Grey Collie. A shared QID or parent pointer does not settle that question. | Resolve the exact VBO/VeNom or registry usage with attributable evidence, explicitly identify the population and conflicting names, and show that the retained portrait and profile concern that same identity. Use scoped attribution for registry disagreement. If evidence only supports uncertainty, preserve the ID and hold; never invent a winning registry or convert a saved cross ID into an unrelated regional breed. |
| Unsupported size or marketing variant | A miniature/teacup label currently lacks an independently defined size population or measurements distinguishing it from existing varieties. | Obtain exact-identity evidence of a bounded variety/selected population and its measurement convention, then write and illustrate that scope. Recognition by a particular registry is optional; a seller's adjective or a smaller generated image alone is insufficient. |
| Remediable source/copy gap, especially anecdotal crosses | Existing prose derives a type's personality from named therapy, rescue, pet-shop, memorial or owner-submission dogs. Those accounts may remain valid individual histories, but do not establish the general profile. | Attempt a concise useful rewrite first. Supported parentage, actual breeding/program history, work context, observed morphology/variation and narrowly attributed firsthand plural experience are admissible; a formal registry, temperament study or predictable inherited personality is not required. Review every generalization against its actual source scope, remove named-dog character as the profile's foundation, add explicit variability, and review the short description and surfaced facts too. If only lexical parentage plus interchangeable boilerplate is available, retain the hold until a specific defensible account is found. Existing approved artwork may remain if it still matches the exact identity; do not require regeneration without a confirmed defect. |

For every held ID, the exact authoring reason is in `data/dogs/catalog-overrides.json#/entities/<ID>/editorialVisibility`. The named originals, source URLs, access limits and rationale for the anecdotal-cross class are in `evidence/cross-dispositions.json`; exact identity conflicts and comparison targets are in `evidence/systemic08-review.json` and `catalog-decisions.json`. These are separate correction records; the original Claude audit is unchanged. Active authoring decisions and final verification take precedence over preliminary worker recommendations in preserved evidence.

## Research and correction attempted within this task

The cross review read all 71 original impacted full/short profiles and source records, compared retained original passages, and screened the combined N content. Before the final salvage pass it prepared nine baseline rewrites: Yorkipoo, Shichon, Springbatt, Majestic Tree Hound, Carlin Pinscher, Cockalier, Pugalier, Springerdoodle and Dakotah Shepherd. Five N crosses had actual breeding-program evidence and were retained; Petite Goldendoodle and Sinhala Hound had distinct standard/population evidence. Mackenzie River Husky received a recorded-freight-history correction through the N review. Thus individual anecdotes did not lead mechanically to hiding the whole class.

Fresh searches and original source reading distinguished breeding-program records and firsthand type observations from generic pages merely inferring behavior from parent breeds. Inaccessible sources were recorded as inaccessible, not false. Some historical originals remained useful; blocked/challenged sources were not repeatedly retried or accessed through another network.

The parent requested an explicit second check that feasible rewrites were not replaced by holds. The root reviewer then found supported Sprocker and Springador replacements in original articles by experienced gundog writers. These emphasize parentage, documented working context, variable morphology and carefully attributed observations rather than guaranteed temperament. A further bounded review checked Sprollie, American Bandogge, Chesador, Morkie, Shih-Poo and Poochon. The lead independently reviewed and applied seven additional rewrites: Sprocker, Springador, American Bandogge, Chesador, Morkie, Shih-Poo and Poochon. Their evidence holds were removed. Sprollie remains held because the bounded review did not establish a useful exact-type account beyond the individually scoped material; this is a residual evidence limitation, not a claim that such evidence cannot exist.

Together, the original 71 anecdotal-cross findings now have 16 supported baseline rewrites and 55 remaining evidence holds. The combined review also retains 16 N cross-class holds, including Coydog’s separate scope issue. Original pre-salvage recommendations remain preserved in historical evidence; the seven guarded amendments and current authoring state record their final supersession.

## What remains unresolved and how restoration works

A hold does not claim exhaustive absence of evidence on the internet. For remaining anecdotal crosses, the unresolved question is whether available exact-type material can support a useful non-boilerplate profile without extrapolating individual animals or parental tendencies. For ambiguous identities, it is which population the source ID means. For redundant or unbounded categories, it is whether an independently useful rankable identity exists at all. These are different problems and must not be collapsed into “unrecognized breed.”

When a reopening test above is met, update the specific authoring record and source evidence, independently review the complete profile/short/facts and artwork, remove only that ID's editorial suppression, and rebuild. Verify the public selection, aliases and stored-state compatibility; do not renumber, merge or delete the old ID. Existing saved rankings/lists reappear through the unchanged ID when eligible. Backups and account payloads retain them during the hold. Previously published public snapshots are historical payloads and are not silently rewritten by this release; newly created/updated snapshots and exports use the current public selection.

No unrelated research cohort, artwork expansion, account change or destructive migration is authorized by these reopening notes. Off-Mac archive backup remains unverified; all original audit material, N checkout, source caches and native masters remain in place.
